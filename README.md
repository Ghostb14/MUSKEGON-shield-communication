# MUSKEGON Shield Communication Aid

> Deter • Detect • Protect

A lightweight library of plain-language, copy-and-edit communication templates for MUSKEGON Shield Security LLC dispatchers, officers, supervisors, and client contacts.

This starter version is intentionally documentation-first. It is not a CAD system, emergency service, substitute for required reports, or source of legal authority.

## Start here

1. Open the template that matches the situation.
2. Replace every `[BRACKETED FIELD]` with verified information.
3. Delete any line that does not apply.
4. Recheck the exact location, current conditions, time, and requested action.
5. Send the message through the approved channel and document it as required.
6. Correct or update the message promptly if the facts change.

Never send a template with unfinished placeholders.

## Communication standard

Use this order whenever possible:

1. **Who:** unit, caller, or authorized contact
2. **Where:** exact address, building, unit, entrance, or landmark
3. **What:** what was seen, heard, or reported
4. **Risk:** known hazards, injuries, weapons, fire, or “unknown”
5. **Action:** what is needed and who has been notified
6. **Confirmation:** acknowledgment, ETA, callback, or next update

Write observable facts in plain language. Separate what an officer personally observed from what another person reported. Avoid slang, unsupported conclusions, unnecessary personal information, and law-enforcement terminology that could misstate private-security authority.

Use 24-hour time unless a client requires another format. Include the local date when a message may be reviewed later.

## Safety and privacy

- Call 911 for an immediate threat to life, active violence, fire, serious medical emergency, or other emergency requiring police, fire, or EMS.
- Follow applicable law, company policy, site post orders, and supervisor direction.
- Do not promise a response time or outcome unless it has been confirmed.
- Do not place access codes, alarm details, protected personal information, tactical plans, or confidential client instructions in public-facing messages or this public repository.
- These templates support communication; they do not replace incident reports, evidence handling, required notifications, or emergency procedures.

## Contributing

Keep additions short, reusable, and easy to read under pressure. Use lowercase, hyphenated filenames and place each template under the audience it serves. Examples must use fictional details and bracketed placeholders.
